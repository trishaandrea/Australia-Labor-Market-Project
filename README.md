# Australian Labour Market: Labour Demand and Labour-Market Slack

## About the Project

This project looks at the Australian labour market from two connected
areas: **labour-market slack** and **labour demand**.

Our aim is to understand what is happening in the labour market and
whether changes in labour demand can give us an early indication of
changes in unused labour capacity.

Rimlan is working on labour-market slack, while Trisha is focusing on
labour demand and job vacancies. We are developing the two parts
separately first so that we can properly understand and validate our
datasets before bringing them together.

The final part of the project will look at whether changes in job
vacancies are followed by changes in unemployment, underemployment or
overall labour underutilisation.

## Research Question

**To what extent can changes in labour demand, measured through job
vacancies, provide an early indication of changes in labour-market
slack in Australia?**

We are treating this as something to investigate using historical data,
rather than assuming that a change in vacancies automatically causes a
change in labour-market slack.

# Bringing the Two Parts Together

Although we are working on different parts of the project, the two
areas are closely connected.

Trisha's work measures **labour demand**, while my work measures
**labour-market slack**. Once both datasets are ready, we can bring them
together and investigate whether changes in job vacancies are followed
by changes in unemployment, underemployment or labour underutilisation.

One area we are particularly interested in is the timing of these
changes.

For example, if job vacancies start falling, does labour-market slack
increase a few months later?

We will investigate these relationships using the data rather than
assuming that one variable causes the other.

# How We Are Approaching the Analysis

We are building the project step by step rather than trying to start
with a complicated model.

### 1. Understand the data

The first step for both parts is making sure we understand exactly what
our data represents.

This includes checking:

- Variable definitions
- Data frequency
- Seasonal adjustment
- Units
- Date coverage
- Missing observations
- Duplicate observations
- Whether related measures are consistent with each other

### 2. Understand labour-market slack

My first stage focuses on unemployment, underemployment and
underutilisation.

A key question is whether unemployment on its own gives us a complete
picture of unused labour capacity.

For example, someone may have a job but still want to work more hours.
This is why underemployment is important when measuring labour-market
slack.

I will compare these measures over time to see how they behave during
different economic periods.
